# A — Extracted research lessons

The earlier teaching-screenshot and mixed-photo research archives were removed from the distributable skill after their reusable lessons were converted to text. Keep these principles; do not keep the source screenshots merely as memory ballast.

## Lighting and tonal structure
- Describe light by **type + direction + visible consequence**, not by a fashionable label alone.
- Strengthen existing directional light only when shadow/reflection evidence supports it.
- Long shadows, edge light, reflections, and silhouette can organize a frame, but they must originate from the photographed scene.
- Overcast scenes should remain overcast; cinematic depth can come from local contrast, tonal separation, restrained color temperature shifts, and shadow density.
- Highlights should stay luminous but textured; darks may be deep but should keep material information unless intentional silhouette is already present.

## Color
- Prefer local warm/cool separation over a global LUT.
- Avoid uniform teal greens, neon cyan skies, over-saturated amber, or one-click orange/teal grading.
- Do not use global saturation/desaturation as the main style mechanism.

## Texture and realism
- Preserve pores, masonry wear, foliage irregularity, water texture, and small photographic imperfections.
- Avoid halos, excessive sharpening, plastic cleanup, fake grain, scratches, bloom, and vignette by default.
- Lower fidelity / native grain can be cinematic when it is already part of the source; do not add it mechanically to clean travel photos.

## Composition and people
- Light/shadow can separate subject from background without fake blur.
- Repetition, architectural geometry, roads, bridge arches, tree shadows, windows, reflections, and silhouettes can carry the composition.
- People are not clutter by definition. Keep them when they add scale, direction, life, or quiet narrative; remove only those that materially compete with the actual subject.

## Tutorial filtering
- Marketing phrases such as “8K”, “HDR”, “cinematic”, “film look”, and “high saturation” are not instructions by themselves.
- External prompts are vocabulary sources, not presets.
- If an edit promises a light/shadow effect, verify that the visible result actually matches the physical scene.
