# C feedback ledger — user-specific portrait rules

## 2026-09-30 — night environmental portrait
User feedback distilled into rules:

- The **person is the final subject**. Do not let the collage/storyboard become the primary subject.
- A successful night portrait may be slightly darker overall and still be better if the person reads clearly.
- Subject prominence should be subtle: small local lift / local contrast plus a quieter background, not visible relighting.
- Night practical light should be **softer and more diffused** than prior attempts; no harsh warm edge on hair/wall.
- Distant structures/lights becoming softer was explicitly liked. Keep depth-based background recession.
- A lower-left environment-only inset was liked. It felt plausible because it repeated the same place/atmosphere without inventing a new person.
- A person-containing inset failed when it showed a different-looking person. Never synthesize a lookalike inset; use same-source crop or environment-only imagery.
- Too many paper, flower, sticker, border, or collage elements made the image feel more AI-generated and reduced the portrait hierarchy.
- Film feeling should come mainly from **light, depth, tonal relation, subtle color, and texture**, not decorative assets.

## 2026-10-01 — whole-person retouch + extra-request layering
- User-specified extras (for example smoother hair, slimmer body/waist) are **additional constraints**, not replacements for C. Always run C's portrait/atmosphere workflow first, then layer the extras.
- “Smoother hair” must be visibly achieved while staying natural: fewer distracting flyaways, clearer strand direction, subtle sheen, no wig-like uniformity.
- “Slimmer” must be visibly achieved while remaining natural, especially at the waist when requested. Prefer clothing drape, silhouette, local shadow, and waist/hip separation before geometric deformation.
- Do not treat newly researched retouch methods as higher-weight rules. They have normal conditional weight and must not override identity, source realism, or existing A/B/C routing principles.
