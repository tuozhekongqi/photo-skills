# Visual Behavior Archetypes v5.1

This intermediate layer sits between `Visual Profile` and `Style Family`. It reduces dependence on subject labels by classifying **how the image behaves**.

An image may have one primary archetype and one secondary archetype.

## V1 — EXPANSIVE_NEGATIVE_SPACE
The image gains power from sky, water, mist, wall, or another quiet field.

Signals:
- high negative space;
- small subject against large field;
- restrained visual rhythm;
- low text tolerance unless the empty area clearly supports it.

Natural families: G, H, J, C.

## V2 — DIRECTIONAL_DEPTH
A path, alley, road, cable, wall, river, railing, or perspective line pulls the eye through depth.

Signals:
- deep perspective;
- strong motion vector;
- one clear directional structure.

Natural families: H, D, C, E.

## V3 — AXIAL_MONUMENTAL
The image is built around symmetry, central axis, stacked architecture, or frontal monumentality.

Signals:
- strong axis;
- strong focal center;
- formal architectural hierarchy.

Natural families: F, I, J, C.

## V4 — REFLECTIVE_DUALITY
Reflection, shadow, glass, or mirrored spatial relationship creates a second reading of the subject.

Signals:
- meaningful reflection or duplicate form;
- upper/lower or real/virtual relationship.

Natural families: G, D, B, C.

## V5 — ORNAMENTAL_RADIAL
The image is organized around a medallion, ceiling, mandala-like ornament, circular or radial repetition.

Signals:
- centered motif;
- radial rhythm;
- high detail extraction value.

Natural families: L, F, I, C.

## V6 — TEXTURAL_DOCUMENTARY
Surface, wear, stone, brick, bark, street material, or lived traces are more important than spectacle.

Signals:
- high texture value;
- documentary mood;
- low need for invented graphics.

Natural families: K, F, E, J.

## V7 — QUIET_SNAPSHOT
The image's strength is its ordinary timing and personal memory rather than formal design.

Signals:
- candid framing;
- human-scale scene;
- emotional authenticity;
- moderate imperfections worth preserving.

Natural families: E, H, J, K.

## V8 — GRAPHIC_SILHOUETTE
One or more bold shapes dominate: tower, roofline, ridge, arch, bridge, building mass, shadow.

Signals:
- strong figure/ground separation;
- clean silhouette;
- limited competing detail.

Natural families: C, J, G, F.

## V9 — LAYERED_HERITAGE
The frame gains meaning from stacked cultural layers: walls, roofs, courtyards, trees, bridges, repeated historical structures.

Signals:
- multi-layer architecture;
- cultural materiality;
- medium-high geometry and texture.

Natural families: I, F, B, J.

## V10 — MOTION_NETWORK
The image is about movement systems: traffic, cable cars, crowds, transit, repeated vehicles, paths, crossings.

Signals:
- repeated moving elements;
- directional rhythm;
- network-like structure.

Natural families: H, D, C, E.

## V11 — COLOR_ANCHOR
One source color carries the composition: red lantern, amber streetlight, turquoise tile, yellow boat, blue-hour skyline.

Signals:
- high color value;
- one dominant accent color;
- removing color would damage meaning.

Natural families: E, J, B, I, A.
Contraindicates K unless a near-monochrome treatment preserves the anchor intentionally.

## V12 — MICRO_DETAIL_SPECIMEN
One crop-worthy detail contains most of the identity: carving, plaque, roof beast, tile, door hardware, ripple, ornament.

Signals:
- high detail extraction value;
- weak need for full-scene context;
- strong material or symbolic specificity.

Natural families: L, F, I, C.

---

## Archetype assignment rule

1. Choose the archetype that best explains the **strongest visual behavior**.
2. Optionally choose one secondary archetype if it adds distinct information.
3. Use archetypes only to suggest one or two plausible treatment directions when helpful; they are not a required ranking stage.
4. Subject category may refine the pool, but may not override a clearly stronger archetype.

Example:
`Great Wall + vast sky` may be `V1 EXPANSIVE_NEGATIVE_SPACE` rather than `heritage`.
`Cable cars over a valley` may be `V2 DIRECTIONAL_DEPTH + V10 MOTION_NETWORK` rather than merely `mountain`.
