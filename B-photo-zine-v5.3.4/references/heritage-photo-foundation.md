# Heritage Photo Foundation for B

Under the shared default route, B follows A for a non-portrait architectural photograph. When the source benefits from the **Heritage Architecture Look**, inherit A’s restrained photographic foundation before deciding how visible the B layer should be.

## Principle
The design layer must sit on top of a convincing photograph. Do not use typography, paper, collage, or warm grading to hide a weak or synthetic base image.

## When visible B treatment should build on A
Keep the default A -> B responsibility order. A visibly expressed heritage/editorial treatment is most useful when:
- the architecture is already strong and deserves careful photographic treatment;
- roof, wall, brick, stone, eave, or axial geometry is important to the visual identity;
- preserving real texture matters;
- a polished photograph and a designed travel page can both add value.

## What B should preserve from the A base
- real weather and light direction;
- authentic roof / wall / stone color relationships;
- readable dark architectural detail;
- historical traces, signage, ornament, and surface wear;
- people that provide useful scale or lived-in atmosphere;
- architectural geometry and perspective.

## Design behavior
- Let architectural structure drive the layout.
- Use typography to occupy real negative space rather than covering key rooflines, plaques, ornament, gates, or stone detail.
- A strong symmetric facade may need very little design.
- A diagonal eave or close architectural detail may work better with sparse text or no text.
- Avoid repeating the same serif-title + torn-paper + handwritten-note formula across every heritage image.
- Do not warm the sky or fabricate sunset merely to make the page feel 'editorial'.

## Quality test
A successful B treatment should make the architecture feel more authored, not less real.
If the graphic layer makes the building look synthetic, generic, or template-driven, reduce the design.
## Positive heritage layout signal — Wall and Heaven
- When the architecture already carries the image, let the photograph remain dominant.
- A title placed outside/below the image can preserve roofline, wall, and sky without visual collision.
- Sparse metadata + one short handwritten line is enough; more copy is usually unnecessary.
- Paper/torn-edge treatment is optional. The transferable lesson is hierarchy, not the exact template.



## A+B clarification
A+B does not require collage or multiple source images. A single strong heritage photograph may receive a minimal A/A-lite finish and then a restrained B editorial layer. Keep the source photograph dominant; do not add extra panels merely to make the result feel designed.
