# Series Planner v5.2

If sequencing matters, a set may benefit from rhythm, but source quality comes first and no diversity target is required.

---

## 1. Visual intensity

Assign each selected output one intensity level:
- `Q` Quiet — large breathing room, low visual density;
- `M` Medium — balanced information and design;
- `S` Strong — concept, graphic intervention, or high visual density.

Use intensity rhythm only as a soft sequencing aid. Repetition is allowed when the photographs genuinely support it. Do not alter good treatments merely to manufacture an alternating pattern.

---

## 2. Sequence roles

Possible roles:
- opener;
- journey / movement;
- breath;
- cultural anchor;
- concept surprise;
- everyday memory;
- closer.

A role is assigned from content and visual behavior, not from style family.

---

## 3. Color rhythm

When ordering a set, avoid accidental color repetition or abrupt visual noise.

Consider:
- warm vs cool;
- dark vs light;
- dense foliage vs open sky;
- red cultural accents vs neutral stone;
- full-color vs monochrome.

A set may intentionally alternate or gradually transition.

---

## 4. Orientation rhythm

Use orientation that best preserves each source. If all outputs must share one social ratio, keep the layout that protects the photograph; do not vary internal layout merely to create diversity.

Do not crop strong horizontals destructively merely to force uniformity. Prefer expansion, paper fields, or framed layouts.

---

## 5. Text rhythm

Text amount follows each photograph. Do not vary copy modes merely to create a pattern. Avoid obvious repeated title placement only when it becomes distracting; otherwise repetition is acceptable.

---

## 6. Final sequence test

View the set as thumbnails. Ask:
- do adjacent images look too similar?
- is there a clear visual pause?
- does one concept dominate?
- does the strongest photo have enough room to lead?
- does the final image feel like a close rather than another random frame?

Reorder before changing good style decisions.

## 7. Same-source series rhythm

For a visually homogeneous batch, do not force output-behavior diversity. Reordering and subtle layout variation are preferred over changing a strong treatment.

Multi-image and experimental interludes are optional only when source relationships clearly justify them. They are not required roles.

Use `batch-diversity.md` for the no-quota, source-led batch policy and the final nine-grid/grid-mosaic failure check.
