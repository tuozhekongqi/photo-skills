# How to filter external teaching material

External tutorials and social-media prompts are evidence, not authority.

### Keep
- preserve original geometry and subject identity;
- specify light direction and the concrete visible result it should produce;
- use shadow/highlight separation to strengthen depth;
- protect highlight detail and textured shadows;
- allow selective warm/cool separation when physically plausible;
- retain skin/material texture;
- evaluate the generated result and revise the one failing light statement instead of rewriting the whole scene.

### Reject or heavily constrain
- "8K/HDR/cinematic" as a substitute for actual photographic logic;
- automatic fog, particles, anamorphic flare, huge sun rays, or background blur;
- global saturation boosts;
- fake film-stock names used as magic words;
- changing weather/time-of-day to manufacture drama;
- adding light sources that were not present;
- turning every shadow into pure black;
- assuming every photo should be warm, high-contrast, or grainy.


### User-feedback correction layer
- Do not treat “remove people” as “empty the scene”; judge each person by visual competition, orientation, edge position, scale contribution, and narrative value.
- Do not let a request for stronger cinematic feeling override weather/time-of-day fidelity. Increase density, hierarchy, and color separation before considering any structural scene change.
- A result that looks attractive but visibly changes the weather, sky structure, foliage identity, architecture, or scene population is still a failed A edit.
- During点评/评价, stop generating and update the feedback ledger first.
