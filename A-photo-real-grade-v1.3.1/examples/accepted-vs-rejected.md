# Accepted vs cautionary anchors

This example file intentionally points only to the **small retained runtime image set**.

## Accepted / positive
- `01_river_dusk_approved.webp`: mood comes from existing sky/water/landscape; no scene rewrite.
- `02_bridge_realistic_approved.webp`: fidelity first; bridge and people remain photographic.
- `03_lake_boats_approved.webp`: restrained atmosphere; boats and shoreline preserved.
- `04_summer_palace_lake_approved.webp`: natural landscape depth; color remains plausible.
- `05_tree_avenue_people_semantic_approved.webp`: people are kept/removed by narrative role, not merely distance.
- `06_tree_bark_insect_approved.webp`: close-up texture and shallow depth can feel cinematic without redesign.
- `07_red_wall_soft_sky_photo_grade_approved.webp`: stronger hierarchy can work when the frame still reads as the same photograph.

## Cautionary archetypes
- `01_invented_light_and_repaint.webp`: synthetic relighting / rewritten appearance.
- `02_overclean_empty_sample_road.webp`: excessive people removal creates sterile AI emptiness.
- `03_full_scene_repaint.webp`: full-frame regeneration destroys source fidelity even if composition is similar.

All secondary/general feedback is text-only in `references/feedback-ledger.md`.
