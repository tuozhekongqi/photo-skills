# Series Planner v5.2

## Strength and photographic-restraint scope

Read `delivery-contract.md` first. Default strong B is independent of photographic T/PRESERVE. Quiet/photo-led means less repainting/clutter, not weaker page design; historical taste cannot lower the current contract.

A strong social-media set should have rhythm, not just diversity.

---

## 1. Visual intensity

Assign each selected output one intensity level:
- `Q` Quiet — large breathing room, low visual density;
- `M` Medium — balanced information and design;
- `S` Strong — concept, graphic intervention, or high visual density.

Avoid sequences such as:
- `S S S S` — exhausting;
- `Q Q Q Q` — flat unless intentionally meditative.

Useful rhythm examples:
- `M Q S Q M S M`
- `S Q M M Q S`

Do not follow a fixed formula; respond to the actual set.

---

## 2. Sequence roles

Possible roles:
- opener;
- journey / movement;
- breath;
- cultural anchor;
- concept surprise;
- everyday memory;
- closer.

A role is assigned from content and visual behavior, not from style family.

---

## 3. Color rhythm

When ordering a set, avoid accidental color repetition or abrupt visual noise.

Consider:
- warm vs cool;
- dark vs light;
- dense foliage vs open sky;
- red cultural accents vs neutral stone;
- full-color vs monochrome.

A set may intentionally alternate or gradually transition.

---

## 4. Orientation rhythm

If the platform permits mixed aspect ratios, use orientation diversity carefully. If all outputs must be the same social ratio, vary internal layout instead.

Do not crop strong horizontals destructively merely to force uniformity. Prefer expansion, paper fields, or framed layouts.

---

## 5. Text rhythm

Across a set, vary:
- no-text image;
- tiny handwritten line;
- restrained editorial title;
- archival caption;
- ticket microcopy.

Do not put a prominent English title in the same corner of every image.

---

## 6. Final sequence test

View the set as thumbnails. Ask:
- do adjacent images look too similar?
- is there a clear visual pause?
- does one concept dominate?
- does the strongest photo have enough room to lead?
- does the final image feel like a close rather than another random frame?

Reorder before changing good style decisions.
