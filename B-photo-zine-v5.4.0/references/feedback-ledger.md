# B — Feedback Ledger

## Historical-feedback scope

The records below remain historical source-fidelity/taste evidence. Older recommendations for a minimum graphic layer, tiny captions or nearly unstyled output do not override the current user-package strong-B delivery contract. Preserve useful photographic restraint and avoid rejected clutter, but implement the required page composition and second functional relation.

B stores feedback **text first**. Only explicit strong positives enter the image library. “还行 / 一般” images are not retained in the runtime package; their useful/weak traits are recorded here.

## Strong / positive directions

- Photo-first hierarchy: the source remains the emotional center; type, paper, collage, and annotations frame it rather than replacing it.
- The approved tree-detail “Small Things” treatment validated a restrained editorial direction: one dominant source image, warm off-white paper, torn edge, elegant serif, short handwritten line, and only a small supporting visual element.
- The approved **Red Wall, Soft Sky** page validates large serif type and calm paper framing when the source offers strong negative space. Learn the large-scale hierarchy and quiet editorial rhythm; do not make warm sky or torn paper mandatory.
- The approved **Beijing / City Notes** page validates a city-note editorial treatment where a strong architecture image stays dominant, text occupies real negative space, and the page feels quiet rather than sticker-heavy.
- Large serif typography works when the source has real space for it, but it is an option, not the house template.
- Quiet travel-editorial language, tactile paper, restrained botanical/archival accents, and sparse copy are positive when visibly motivated by the source.

## “还行” lessons — text only

- **Quiet Gate:** symmetrical architecture + title avoiding the central subject worked. Weakness: obvious template repetition and weather warming. Keep the symmetry/spacing lesson; discard the compulsory sunset/paper recipe.
- **Walls of Silence:** large building mass + upper-left title + upper-right short note gave usable balance. Weakness: same template family and artificial warm-sky tendency.
- “还行” means partial approval only. Extract the useful layout behavior; do not add the image to the positive library.

## “一般” lessons — diagnosis only

- **Corridor of Time:** too much repetition of `large serif + off-white paper + torn edge + handwritten sentence + warm sky`; insufficient response to the source's long perspective, grey masonry, red wall, and trench geometry.
- B must design from the photo’s strongest behavior first. For a long corridor/trench image, geometry, direction, brick rhythm, and axial depth should determine the page before typography does.
- “一般” outputs should not remain as image references. Their diagnosis belongs in text.

## Cautionary / negative patterns

- Do not reuse the same `large serif + torn paper + warm sunset sky + handwritten quote` composition across unrelated sources. A liked treatment becomes stale when repeated mechanically.
- B may redesign layout, but should not casually repaint source weather or architecture to fit a prettier template. An overcast photograph can remain overcast and still become a strong zine page.
- Do not make every historical-architecture image look like the same premium travel-magazine cover. Vary family, layout signature, text amount, crop logic, material treatment, and source-to-paper relationship.
- Avoid generic poetic copy detached from the visible scene.
- If A-grade realism already carries the image, B should add only the minimum graphic layer needed.

## Current default target

B should feel like an art director built a page **around this specific photograph**, not like a template generator converted every photograph into the same branded poster.
